Day-before install — how the Lougheed corridor shapes scheduling

A 20×60 marquee staged on a Hatzic Valley acreage — mountain backdrop framing the install.

A Mission marquee is structurally identical to a Lower Mainland marquee. What changes is install timing. The 75-minute Lougheed corridor delivery means we can’t realistically install Saturday morning and clear Saturday afternoon — too much crew time burns on the corridor for a same-day install to be efficient.

Standard Mission Saturday-event schedule:

  • Friday afternoon: install crew arrives, marquee up by end-of-day, anchored for overnight.
  • Saturday morning: chair and table layout, lighting strands, final floor-plan refinement.
  • Saturday event.
  • Sunday morning: teardown crew runs the corridor again, marquee back at the warehouse before late-Sunday traffic.

This is the same install template we use for Chilliwack and Harrison Hot Springs — the longer-haul Eastern Fraser Valley cities. It costs no more than a half-day urban install because the corridor time gets absorbed into a single overnight rather than two same-day runs.

Hatzic Valley and Hatzic Lake acreage

A frame tent staked on open acreage, mountains behind.

Hatzic Valley acreage weddings drive the upscale Mission marquee calendar. Most are 100–150 guests under a 20×60 marquee on the acreage lawn. Hatzic Lake-adjacent properties run an additional anchor consideration — sidewalls year-round given the lake-side onshore breeze.

Engineered ground stakes are the default; supplemental ballast at the lake-facing or valley-facing corners where the wind line concentrates.

Silverhill and Cedar Valley — valley-view acreage

Silverhill and Cedar Valley sit higher up the Fraser Valley with longer driveways, more pronounced mountain-and-valley views, and (in some cases) significant lot slope. Slope-aware anchor strategy is the default — supplemental downhill ballast for grades steeper than 5%, engineered slope-staking where the lawn has been pre-graded for events.

The marquee placement itself becomes a photographic decision at these venues — the view is the anchor, and we walk the lot during the pre-quote site visit to identify the orientation that frames the valley best.

Fraser River Heritage Park

Fraser River Heritage Park’s ceremony zones run on heritage permits with venue-coordinated delivery. Most ceremony marquees are 20×30 to 20×40; full reception marquees rarely fit the permitted setbacks. The standard pattern: smaller ceremony marquee at Heritage Park, reception marquee at a Hatzic or Silverhill acreage with the full equipment line.

Mission Leisure Centre and downtown supplemental

Mission Leisure Centre and downtown community-venue events occasionally book supplemental marquees for outdoor cocktail-hour cover or registration overflow. 20×20 to 20×40 footprints; hardscape installs use full ballast.

Hatzic Prairie and rural-edge acreage

The rural-edge acreage areas around Hatzic Prairie and the eastern Mission lots have the largest single Mission marquee bookings — 30×60 marquees for 200-guest weddings are routine here. Driveway approach measured in hundreds of metres; truck-stage distance factors into the crew-time line.

A 100-guest Mission tent rental order

For a 100-guest Mission wedding — say a Saturday in August at a Hatzic Valley acreage with ceremony and reception under a single marquee — a typical tent-side order looks like:

  • 1 × 20×60 marquee tent (covered ceremony + reception in single footprint)
  • 4 × tent sidewalls (full enclosure for evening)
  • 8 × bistro string-light strands across the marquee ridge
  • 1 × tent heater (October–April bookings; optional in summer Fraser Valley evenings)
  • Engineered ground stakes (default for Hatzic acreage lawn; supplemental ballast on lake-adjacent or windward corners)

Setup runs 5–6 hours for a 20×60 + sidewall configuration; teardown is 3–4 hours. Both quoted up front. The 50/100/150/200 rental checklist maps the marquee sizing to your headcount. Mission Tent Rentals FAQ

Do you charge a Mission-distance premium?
No — the Lougheed corridor delivery is itemised as a line on the quote, but there's no Fraser Valley uplift on the per-tent rate. The Saturday-event install timing absorbs the corridor logistics; we install Friday afternoon and the marquee stays anchored overnight.
What's the standard install for a Hatzic Valley acreage?
20×60 marquee on the acreage lawn (sometimes 30×60 for 150-guest receptions), engineered ground stakes, sidewalls year-round (Mission afternoon valley wind is real), bistro lighting along the ridge, tent heater October–April. Setup runs 5–6 hours; teardown is 3–4. Driveway grading and irrigation are confirmed during the pre-quote walk.
Can you install at Fraser River Heritage Park?
Yes — Fraser River Heritage Park's ceremony zones run on heritage permits with venue-coordinated delivery windows. Most ceremony marquees are 20×30 or 20×40 — the park's setbacks rarely accommodate full reception tents. Stakes plus supplemental ballast at the river-facing corners; sidewalls year-round given the river-side onshore breeze.
How do you handle Silverhill or Cedar Valley hillside acreages?
Slope-aware anchor strategy is the default. Most Silverhill and Cedar Valley acreages have graded lawns suitable for stake-down installs; the few with significant slope get engineered slope-staking or supplemental downhill ballast. We walk the site during quoting if the slope is non-standard. We don't install on slopes that need engineered platforms unless one already exists.
Can you do an abbey-view or Westminster Abbey-adjacent install?
Westminster Abbey itself doesn't host weddings, but several Mission venues and private residences with abbey-view sightlines do. Standard install patterns apply — the marquee is sized to the headcount; the view is the photographic anchor. Most abbey-view residences accommodate a 20×40 to 20×60 marquee comfortably.
