## What a New Westminster marquee install actually involves

![A clean white marquee tent set against a vibrant blue sky with fluffy clouds](/images/lifestyle/tent-golden-hour-2.jpg)
*A 20×40 marquee staged on grass — Pier Park, Queen's Park, and Queensborough patterns share the same install template.*

New West's compressed geography means the install conversation is venue-specific in a way most cities aren't. Five operational patterns cover almost every booking:

- **Pier Park and Quay** — Fraser River wind, city event permits, ballast on hardscape, stakes plus ballast on the lawn zones. Sidewalls year-round at every waterfront install.
- **Queen's Park ceremony** — Heritage trees and protected sightlines. 20×20 to 20×40 footprints depending on zone. Overhead-branch clearance measured during the pre-quote walk.
- **Sapperton and downtown heritage** — Tight truck access, smaller backyard footprints (20×30 typical), pre-stage closer to the property line.
- **Queensborough lofts** — Building is usually the venue; marquees are supplemental for outdoor cocktail-hour. 10×10 popups and 20×20s dominate.
- **Uptown civic and Anvil Centre** — Permit-coordinated installs on civic plazas. 20×40 standard for ceremony or supplemental reception zones.

## Fraser-side wind and ballast strategy

Pier Park's exposure to Fraser River afternoon wind is consistent year-round. Stakes work on the lawn zones, but we always pair them with supplemental ballast at the corners that face the river. On the hardscape sections (the pier itself, the Quay's waterfront promenade), it's ballast-only — concrete weights, not water-fillable.

The 5–6 hour install window on a Pier Park 20×60 includes the time to walk the perimeter and position ballast against the wind line. Other vendors sometimes skip that step. We don't.

## Queen's Park heritage trees and clearance

Queen's Park's ceremony zones run on protected-sightline rules that vary by specific zone. Most accept a 20×20 cocktail tent or 20×30 supplemental marquee, but a 20×60 reception tent rarely fits the available footprint within the permitted setbacks.

The standard New West wedding pattern when Queen's Park is the ceremony venue: ceremony chairs only at Queen's Park (no marquee — heritage tree canopy provides the cover), and the marquee installed at the reception venue, which is typically a Sapperton restaurant lawn, an Anvil Centre courtyard, or a Queensborough loft outdoor space.

## Sapperton, Downtown, and Uptown heritage streets

Sapperton's heritage residential streets and the downtown core have meaningfully tighter truck access than Queensborough or the suburban edges. We use shorter delivery windows for these installs and pre-stage closer to the property line. The marquee itself doesn't change; the install logistics do.

Most Sapperton residential backyards accept a 20×30 comfortably. Downtown heritage lots are smaller — 20×20 cocktail tents or 10×10 popups for the cocktail-hour cover are more common than full reception marquees.

## Queensborough industrial-loft conversions

Queensborough's industrial-loft conversion venues have grown into a regular share of the New West wedding calendar. Most lofts don't need a marquee — the building is the venue. Where they do book a marquee, it's for the outdoor cocktail-hour space or a 20×20 supplemental footprint adjacent to the loading dock.

## A 100-guest New Westminster tent rental order

For a 100-guest New West wedding — say a Saturday in August with ceremony at Queen's Park and reception at a Sapperton restaurant lawn — a typical tent-side order looks like:

- 1 × **20×60 marquee tent** at the reception venue (covered reception; ceremony is open-air at Queen's Park under tree canopy)
- 4 × **tent sidewalls** (full enclosure for evening)
- 6 × **bistro string-light strands** across the marquee ridge
